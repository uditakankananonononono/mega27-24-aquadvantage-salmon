# Independent salmon heat paper: matched biology, unmatched measurement

The [2014 Atlantic salmon heat/oxygen paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4046827/) is an independent population and an informative challenge to the idea that any salmon temperature study is a valid held-out test. Its chronic heat exposure is 19°C, alongside a low-oxygen arm (4-5 mg/L); the authors say they used pooled samples and two different cDNA library methods, did not compare the libraries quantitatively, and consider their RNA-seq indicative rather than an exact quantitative treatment measure. The [2022 transgenic salmon paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168996/) instead studies 18 liver RNA-seq libraries from female triploid growth-hormone-transgenic Atlantic salmon at 10.5/13.5/16.5°C. Cross-paper sameness of species and broad heat biology does not establish same line, exposure, assay or expression-estimation task.

`results/independent_heat_cohort_screen.json` records verbatim source passages and SHA-256s; `scripts/screen_independent_heat_cohort.py` and its test replay the source check. We retain the 2014 paper as background and an explicit negative eligibility example, not a held-out same-task cohort. No gene-level cross-validation, independent product-level claim, raw expression analysis, service/accession/derivation/page gate or novelty credit follows. An independently held-out *comparable* data source and fair strongest-comparator design remain open. Screening an attractive near-match out now prevents a false validation claim later.
