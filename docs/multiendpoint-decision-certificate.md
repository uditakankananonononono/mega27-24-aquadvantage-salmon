# Salmon endpoint-conflict decision certificate, first prototype

The [2019 AquAdvantage salmon thesis](https://memorial.scholaris.ca/bitstreams/a668d444-72ba-4897-91b9-d8b38ac6257d/download) prints group means at 10.5, 13.5 and 16.5 °C for days to reach 1500 g (475, 418, 382), late-interval FCR (0.86, 0.87, 1.01) and late-interval TGC (2.09, 1.86, 1.43). A days-only rule picks 16.5 °C. Our prototype instead makes the objective conflict explicit: **all three groups are Pareto nondominated** across lower days, lower FCR and higher TGC. On a prespecified three-weight grid (0.01 increments, 5,151 combinations), min-max-normalized descriptive winner counts were 2,199 for 10.5 °C, 2,372 for 13.5 °C and 580 for 16.5 °C. These counts are **not** probabilities, prevalence estimates, statistical confidence or a recommended farm policy; weights were not elicited from a decision maker. They show why a single 'best' treatment needs an explicit objective.

The candidate invention is a provenance-aware abstaining certificate that requires objective choice, identifies source-unit hierarchy and refuses causal or welfare optimization from aggregate means. Pareto dominance, normalization and weighted utility are established methods, not new algorithms; [aquaculture multi-criteria decision work](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10313446) is relevant prior art. The three means came from the same cohort and were known before this prototype, with no individual fish trajectories, tank-to-run crosswalk or independent validation. It cannot separate temperature from differing time-to-size, and there is no fair strongest-comparator win, biological discovery or gate credit.
