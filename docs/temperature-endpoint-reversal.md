# AquAdvantage salmon growth rankings depend on the endpoint

[Ignatz's 2019 master's thesis](https://memorial.scholaris.ca/items/eef8b6b0-8b69-499a-a6a3-2bf0e1567743) reports an AquAdvantage Salmon female triploid freshwater rearing trial at 10.5°C, 13.5°C and 16.5°C. In the author's growth-results passage, groups reached a mean 1500 g in **475, 418 and 382 days**, respectively. The warmest group reached the target **93 days sooner** than the coolest. Yet [the full thesis PDF, Table 2-2](https://memorial.scholaris.ca/bitstreams/a668d444-72ba-4897-91b9-d8b38ac6257d/download) reports 800-to-1500 g thermal-unit growth coefficients of **2.09, 1.86 and 1.43**, and feed conversion ratios **0.86, 0.87 and 1.01**, respectively. Faster calendar time in warmth is consistent with *lower* temperature-normalized growth and *worse* late-interval feed conversion. The endpoints answer different questions; this is an author-reported pattern, not our independent discovery or a verdict that one temperature is best.

The [2022 liver RNA-seq article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168996/) cites Ignatz et al.'s related growth work and samples size-matched fish from the broader temperature experiment. Consequently the thesis is **not an independent held-out validation cohort** for transcriptomic predictions. The authors' Table 2-2 gives means ±SEM by group, but this audit did not extract individual fish trajectories or measure a new model against any comparator. We re-read the archived original 169-page thesis PDF, pin its SHA-256 and check exact source passage and table rows in a replay test. No sequence/construct optimization, product-wide safety claim, accession payload, audited derivation, paper-page gate or novel win follows. A decision model would have to state whether calendar time, feed use, nutrient retention or welfare is the objective before training or comparing methods.
